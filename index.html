FROM THE DESK OF THE REGIONAL MANAGER

RE: Threat Level Trivia....and Why?

TO: The Interwebs

It all started by attending an Office trivia night, apparently trivia nights have become quite 'a thing' - and it appears The Office themed events are always packed. When I attended my first...Standing room only. That part was great. The questions? Not so much.

They were the kind of questions where, if you know just enough, you get them right, but if you actually know the show deeply, you start raising your hand to dispute the answer. The kind where the wording leaves a crack open for a technically-correct-but-obscure counterpoint, and instead of celebrating what you know, you're arguing with a pleasant and kind host, who's hardly watched the show, about Season 4 continuity. That's not trivia. That's frustration with a microphone.

And look, I'm not trying to be difficult. I don't want to be the person standing up in a crowded bar yelling "ACTUALLY, Dwight said that in Season 3, not Season 2." But if you're going to ask me trivia about a show I've watched enough times to qualify as a dependent on my taxes, the answers need to be right.

So this became the thing: There needs to be a better Office trivia question bank. Not just a casual quiz you click through once on some clickbait site between listicles about which character is your spirit animal. A real, curated, community-validated collection of questions that hold up under scrutiny from people who have seen the show way more times than they probably should admit. We're talking Dinner Party episode uncomfortable levels of scrutiny.

Threat Level Trivia is that project. Named after the legendary Michael Scott film Threat Level Midnight (but just different enough to keep the lawyers at bay), it's a free, browser-based trivia game built for the kind of fan who appreciates accuracy.

The game has solo mode for those lone wolves out there (Dwight would approve - he works best alone, obviously), party mode for groups who want to settle debates the right way, difficulty tiers ranging from "Casual Friday" to "basically a Schrute family hazing ritual," and most importantly, a community feedback loop. Every question can be rated for difficulty and flagged for disputes, because this isn't a top-down trivia dictatorship. It's more of a "suggestion box that people actually read" situation. Unlike a certain suggestion box at a certain paper company that was mostly used for complaints about a certain regional manager.

The best Office trivia should be shaped by the people who care about it the most. There is always somebody who knows more than you do about any given thing, and this project is built to welcome exactly those people. You know the name of the bat that got loose in the office? You remember what Ryan's business degree thesis was about? You can recite the rules of Flonkerton? Welcome home.

It's a fan-made labor of love, built one question at a time, validated by the community, and served up in the aesthetic of Scranton's finest paper company. No question goes in without being held to a standard, and no answer gets to hide behind lazy wording. We spent a lot of late nights making sure every single one could handle whatever you throw at it - and trust me, it's harder than it looks. That's what she said.

If you think you know The Office, prove it. And if you find a question to dispute...Great! That's the whole point. File a complaint, rate the difficulty, tell us we're wrong. This project runs on that kind of passion. As the great Wayne Gretzky, as quoted by the great Michael Scott, once said: "You miss 100% of the shots you don't take."

So take the shot.

Threat Level Trivia is a fan project and is not affiliated with NBC, Universal Television, or any related entities.

How to Play

Question of the Day

The home screen leads with a single trivia question that's the same for every visitor that day. It resets at your local midnight, so the day boundary matches the day you're actually in, not UTC.

Pick an answer and the card flips to a result panel showing whether you nailed it, the correct answer, an Office quote for your reaction, and your current streak. After that, the daily is locked until tomorrow.

Streaks: Get the daily right two days in a row and your streak goes up. Get it wrong, or miss a day, and your streak resets to zero. The flame icon in the daily card is your current streak; your longest streak shows next to it once you've topped your previous best. Streaks are stored in your browser, so they live and die with this device.

Solo Mode - "Begin the Deposition"

Test your Office knowledge on your own. Enter your name, pick a category, difficulty, and how many questions you want. Answer multiple-choice questions (or go Hardcore and type your own answers).

Employee Records (Global Leaderboard)

The global leaderboard tracks the best scores across all players. To qualify, you must complete 25 questions on Medium or Hard difficulty. Easy questions and Mixed difficulty do not qualify. This keeps the rankings honest - no cherry-picking easy questions or stopping at 5 to lock in a perfect score.

Use the Difficulty filter at the top of the leaderboard to view All, Medium only, or Hard only. Ranks recompute for the filtered view, so you can see exactly where you stand against just the Medium field or just the Hard field.

Party Mode - "Call a Meeting"

Grab 2-8 players for a group showdown. Everyone takes turns answering questions on the same screen. Pick a Speed Round timer (30, 20, or 15 seconds) to play for bonus points, or leave it Off for a relaxed pace. When a speed round is on, a 3-2-1-GO countdown runs before each question so no one peeks early; with speed off, there's no countdown. If there's a tie at the end, a sudden-death wager round settles it.

Challenge Mode - "Send a Fax to Friends"

Not everyone can make it to the conference room. Challenge Mode lets you compete with friends, coworkers, or strangers anywhere in the world without being in the same place at the same time.

How it works:

  • Pick your settings (category, difficulty, question count, and an optional speed round of 30, 15, or 10 seconds - 10 is "Ludicrous") and hit "Create & Copy Memo Link."
  • The game locks in a specific set of questions and generates a shareable link with a unique memo code.
  • Send that link to anyone - text it, email it, post it in a group chat, whatever works.
  • Every person who opens the link plays the exact same questions in the exact same order with the same answer options. No randomness, no advantage. A level playing field.
  • After finishing, everyone's score lands on a shared per-memo leaderboard so you can see exactly how you stack up.

You can reshare the same memo as many times as you want, or create a new one whenever you feel like starting fresh. It's great for settling debates, running an office competition, or just proving to your group chat that you know more about The Office than they do.

Difficulty Levels

  • Easy: If you've seen the show, you'll probably get these. General characters, big moments, famous quotes.
  • Medium: You've watched a few times through. Specific episodes, side characters, running gags.
  • Hard: Deep cuts. Background details, exact quotes, obscure episode facts. You basically live in Scranton.
  • Random: A mix of everything. Good luck.

Hardcore Mode

No multiple choice. The question appears and you have to know the answer before clicking "Reveal." Then you honestly score yourself. It's the honor system - something Dwight would never trust, but we're going with it anyway.

This Is a Community-Driven Question Bank

This isn't a trivia game where someone wrote 200 questions and walked away. The question bank is a living thing, shaped by the people who play it. Every vote, every rating, every dispute, and every submitted question makes the game better for the next person. That's the whole point.

If you play a round and something feels off, say something. If you know an obscure fact that would make a perfect question, send it in. If a question nails it, give it a thumbs up. The best Office trivia should come from the people who care about it the most, and that's you.

Vote on Questions

After every answer in solo and challenge modes, you'll see thumbs up and thumbs down buttons. This is your way of telling us whether a question is good - well written, accurate, and fun to answer - or whether it needs work. Questions that consistently get downvoted get flagged for review. One tap, real impact.

Rate Difficulty

Below the vote buttons there's a difficulty slider from 1 to 10. Slide it to tell us how hard you thought the question actually was, regardless of what label it has. Once enough players rate a question, the community average overrides the original difficulty label in gameplay. If we tagged something as "Easy" and the community says it's an 8 out of 10, the game adjusts. Crowdsourced accuracy.

Question Feedback / Disputes

Think the answer is wrong? Wording is misleading? The question is outdated or doesn't account for something? Hit the "Question Feedback / Dispute" link below the answer and tell us exactly what's off. Every single submission gets reviewed. If you're right, we fix it. If it's a gray area, we'll tighten the wording. This is how the question bank stays honest.

Submit a Trivia Question

Think you've got a stumper the world needs to see? Hit the Suggestion Box on the home screen, then click "Submit it here" to open the question submission form. You'll fill out the question text, the correct answer, three wrong answers, category, difficulty, and optional context explaining why the answer is right. Every submission lands in a review queue where it gets polished and verified before it goes live. If your question makes the cut, it becomes part of the official bank that every player sees. Your knowledge, permanently baked into the game.

Suggestion Box

Got a feature idea, a bug report, general feedback, or just want to tell us your favorite Michael Scott moment? The Suggestion Box on the home screen is always open. We read every message. Seriously, every one.

v1.8.0 2026-06-06
Fixes
  • Admin Approve/Dismiss Now Sticks: Fixed a long-standing bug where flagged-question approvals or dismissals appeared to take effect during the session but reverted on the next visit, on another device, or after a session restart. Both the player flow (filing a new dispute) and the admin flow (approve/dismiss/delete) were POSTing the entire disputes array to the server, which blindly overwrote the file - so whichever side wrote last clobbered the other side's change. The same broken pattern affected votes and ratings (less visibly). Now players append one item at a time and admins mutate by id server-side under a thread lock, so concurrent writes can't overwrite each other. Admin panels also re-pull live state from the server on open and after every action, so an admin never starts from a stale localStorage snapshot.
Features
  • Question of the Day (Wordle-style): The home screen now leads with a single daily trivia question pulled from the same bank as the rest of the game. Every visitor on the same calendar day sees the same question, picked deterministically by the days-since-launch index. Resets at the user's local midnight (not UTC) so the day boundary matches their actual day. After answering, the prompt flips to a result card with whether they were right, the correct answer, a themed Office quote, and their updated streak, then locks for the rest of the day.
  • Streak Tracking: A flame-icon streak chip in the daily card shows the visitor's current streak (and longest, when it's higher). Correct + previous correct was yesterday increments the streak. Wrong, or a gap of more than a day, resets it to zero. Stored in localStorage along with a per-user history log (date / question ID / right or wrong), capped at 365 entries.
  • Anonymous Retention Logging: Each daily result is reported to the server with an anonymous persistent visitor ID (generated once per browser, not tied to any name or email), so retention can be measured across all visitors instead of being trapped per-browser.
  • Admin "Daily Question" Panel: New admin tab showing how many people answered each day, the daily correct/incorrect split with a percent-correct bar, headline totals (days tracked, total submissions, unique visitors, longest streak observed), and a current-streak distribution histogram (binned 0 / 1 / 2 / 3-6 / 7-13 / 14-29 / 30+). Includes a Refresh button to pull the latest counts without leaving the panel.
Infrastructure
  • New data/daily-stats.json store. GET /api/daily-stats serves the aggregate; player-facing POST /api/daily-stats records one anonymous result {date, questionId, correct, visitorId, streak, longest} under a new _daily_lock, validating with a strict date regex, visitor-ID regex, and bounded ints. Writes go to a temp file then os.replace for atomicity.
  • New js/daily.js module, fully separate from GameState so the daily flow can never interfere with solo/party/challenge play. Eligible pool is the active managed bank (base + custom, minus deleted/disabled) sorted by ID; today's question is pool[daysSinceLaunch % poolLen]. All date math runs in local time. Daily answers also flow into the existing per-question Answer Stats tally via the shared recordAnswerStat() chokepoint.
  • Home-screen intro copy refreshed to mention the daily question alongside solo, party, and challenge.
  • Disputes/Votes/Ratings: per-item endpoints under a new _player_data_lock. The old whole-array overwrite on POST /api/disputes / /api/votes / /api/ratings is replaced with server-side appends (votes also dedupe by (player, questionId)). Admin actions go through dedicated per-id endpoints: POST /api/admin/disputes/status, /api/admin/disputes/delete, /api/admin/ratings/reset, /api/admin/votes/reset. All writes are atomic (temp file + os.replace) and serialized through the lock.
  • Client-side: addDispute, addRating, addVote POST one item; updateDisputeStatus, deleteDispute, resetQuestionRatings, resetQuestionVotes use the new admin per-id endpoints with the admin token. New reloadDisputesFromFile() / reloadRatingsFromFile() / reloadVotesFromFile() helpers in js/state.js overwrite localStorage with the live server state; the admin panel switcher, admin login, and the resolve/remove handlers all call these so the admin view is always sourced from the server file.
v1.7.0 2026-06-03
Features
  • Exclude "Behind the Scenes" Questions: A checkbox under the Category dropdown in all three lobbies (solo, party, challenge) lets players leave out actor/production/cast-trivia questions and play only with knowledge you'd get from watching the show. The live pool count updates as you toggle it, and it's auto-ignored when you've explicitly picked the Behind the Scenes category.
  • Leaderboard Ranking & Per-Difficulty Storage: Hard and Medium are now separate ladders that each hold up to 100 entries (instead of both fighting over one shared top 100), so a strong Medium run is never bumped off by Hard runs. Within a ladder, entries rank by score (highest first), then full-bank runs above ones that excluded Behind the Scenes, then most recent - a newer run wins an otherwise-equal tie, rewarding players who keep coming back. Hard always ranks above Medium, never blended by raw score. The "All" view stacks the board Hard-first then Medium below (up to 200); the Medium / Hard tabs each show up to 100.
  • Leaderboard Reserve (300-Score Retention): The public board shows the top 100 per difficulty, but storage now retains up to 300 scores total instead of dropping everything past the cut. Scores demoted below a tier's top 100 are kept "in reserve" (only the lowest-ranked drop once 300 is exceeded), so they flow back onto the board if the display limit is raised later. The admin Leaderboard tab lists every saved score and marks reserve ones with an amber "In reserve - not shown" pill plus a saved / on-board / in-reserve summary.
  • "Excluded Behind the Scenes" Leaderboard Note: When a player posts a qualifying score with Behind the Scenes excluded, a small italic note appears under their name on the global leaderboard (and on their own results card), so a score is never quietly assumed to have come from the full question bank.
  • Answer Stats (Admin): A new Answer Stats panel logs every answer across solo, party, and challenge play. Each question shows how many times it was answered correctly, how many times wrong, the total, and a percent-correct bar. Search, filter by category/difficulty, and sort by most/least answered or lowest/highest percent correct. Reset a single question's counters or all of them.
Infrastructure
  • New data/answer-stats.json store with GET /api/answer-stats, a player-facing POST /api/answer-stats that atomically increments a question's correct/wrong tally (thread-locked so concurrent players never lose a count), and an admin POST /api/admin/answer-stats/reset for one question or all.
  • filterQuestions() / selectQuestions() take an excludeBts flag; recordAnswerStat() in js/state.js fires from the shared scoreAnswer() chokepoint plus the party speed-round timeout.
  • Leaderboard submissions now carry an excludeBts flag. The server ranks entries by score → full-bank-before-excluded → most-recent (reusing each entry's millisecond id timestamp for recency, so no new field), and retains every displayed entry (top 100 per tier) plus the best overflow up to a 300-total cap. The client caps each tier at 100 on display (_lbTopOfTier) so reserve entries never leak to the public board; the admin Leaderboard view flags anything past a tier's top 100 as "in reserve."
  • Fixed a latent server bug where the request logger crashed the connection on any error response (it assumed a log argument was the request path, but error logging passes a status code). Every API error path is now safe.

Admin Guide

A quick reference for everything in the admin panel. If you're reading this, congratulations - you have more power than Dwight ever did. Use it wisely.

Questions

The full question bank. Every question in the game lives here.

  • Question ID: Each card shows its question ID ("Question ID: 123") in the top-left in small italic text. The same ID also appears at the top of the question editor when you open a card, on Community Ratings cards, and on dispute cards. Handy for cross-referencing across disputes, exports, and ratings.
  • Search & Filter: Narrow down by keyword, category, or difficulty. The count updates in real time. When the search box has text, hover (or focus) it to reveal a clear (x) button that empties it in one click.
  • Edit: Opens the question editor where you can change the question text, answer, category, difficulty, and tags. Edits to built-in questions are stored as overrides - the original is preserved underneath and the card gets an "EDITED" badge.
  • Disable/Enable: Disabling a question hides it from gameplay without removing it. Useful for pulling a bad question while you fix it. Reversible at any time.
  • Revert: Appears on edited built-in questions. Strips your edit overlay and restores the original question text. Your edits will be lost.
  • Delete: Permanently removes the question from gameplay and the admin panel. Custom questions are fully erased; built-in questions are added to a separate "deleted" list (since they're baked into the game's source). Either way, this cannot be undone from the UI.
  • Add New Question: Creates a custom question that gets added to the pool. Custom questions are tagged with a "CUSTOM" badge so you can tell them apart from the built-in set.
  • Tags: Optional labels on questions (like character names or episode references). Players can filter by character in the lobby, and tags power that filtering.

Suggested Questions

Questions submitted by players through the Suggestion Box. Each suggestion shows the full question, answer options, category, and difficulty.

  • Review & Revise: Opens the suggestion in the question editor so you can review the full text and make any edits (grammar, capitalization, distractor tweaks) before approving. The suggestion is only added to the question bank when you click "Add to Question Bank" inside the editor. Closing or canceling leaves it pending.
  • Review Later: Marks the suggestion as deferred so you can come back to it.
  • Delete: Marks the suggestion as rejected. It stays in the list but grays out.
  • Remove: Appears on resolved (approved or rejected) cards. Permanently deletes the suggestion from the list entirely. This cannot be undone.

Flagged Questions

When a player submits "Question Feedback / Dispute" during gameplay, the entry shows up here. Each card shows the question, the question ID, the player's comment, and when it was filed. You can mark them as Approved (the dispute is valid and you'll fix it) or Dismissed (the question is fine as-is). These status changes are just labels for your tracking - they don't automatically modify the question. Use the "Edit Question" shortcut on the card to jump straight into the question editor. Once a dispute is resolved (approved or dismissed) it grays out, and a Remove button appears to permanently delete it from the list.

Behind the scenes: Approve / Dismiss / Remove each go through a per-id endpoint to the server (no more "send the whole list back" - a previous version of this could let a freshly-filed player dispute silently overwrite your status change). The panel also re-pulls the live state from the server every time you open it and after every action, so you're always working off the canonical list, not whatever was cached in this browser. If you ever notice an action not sticking, hop into a different tab or device and reload the panel - what you see there is the source of truth.

Submitted Feedback

General messages submitted through the Suggestion Box on the home screen (general feedback, feature ideas, bug reports, etc.). Each card has a Remove button to permanently delete that entry. Player-submitted trivia questions have their own dedicated "Suggested Questions" tab.

Community Ratings

Shows how players are rating and voting on questions.

  • Difficulty Ratings: Players rate each question's difficulty on a 1-10 scale after answering. Once a question gets enough ratings (currently 3+), the community average overrides the original difficulty label in gameplay. The "Overriding" badge means the community score is active.
  • Votes (Upvotes/Downvotes): Players can thumbs-up or thumbs-down each question. Green numbers are upvotes, red are downvotes. A "Needs Review" badge appears when a question has 5+ total votes and more downvotes than upvotes - that's a signal the community thinks the question has a problem.
  • Reset Ratings / Reset Votes: Clears all community data for that specific question. Useful after you've edited a question and want fresh feedback.
  • Filters: "More Downvotes Than Upvotes" is the quickest way to find problem questions that need attention.

Answer Stats

Every time a player answers a question - in solo, party, or challenge mode - the result is tallied here. This is your read on which questions players nail and which ones trip them up.

  • Per-question counts: Each card shows how many times the question was answered correctly (✓), how many times wrong (✗), the total, and a percent-correct bar. A low percentage on an "Easy" question is a good hint the question (or its answer) might need a second look.
  • What counts: Multiple-choice answers, Hardcore Mode self-scores, and party speed-round timeouts (counted as wrong) all tally. The headline row at the top sums everything: how many questions have been answered at least once, total answers logged, and the overall correct rate.
  • Search, Filter & Sort: Narrow by keyword, category, or difficulty. Sort by most/least answered, or by lowest/highest percent correct - "Lowest % Correct" surfaces the questions players struggle with most. Questions never answered yet sink to the bottom.
  • Reset: Each answered question has a Reset button to zero its counters (handy after you edit a question and want a fresh read). Reset All wipes every counter across the whole bank. Both are permanent.
  • Refresh: The list loads when you open the tab. Hit Refresh to pull the latest tallies without leaving the panel.

Daily Question

Anonymous tally of how visitors engage with the home-screen Question of the Day. This is the read on whether the daily mechanic is bringing people back.

  • Headline totals: Days tracked, total submissions, unique visitors, and the longest current streak observed across all visitors. Plus the overall correct percentage for context.
  • Current Streak Distribution: A histogram binning each unique visitor by their most recent streak: 0 (broken or never correct), 1, 2, 3-6, 7-13, 14-29, 30+ days. Bars are scaled to the largest bin so smaller bins stay visible. Each row shows the count and the percent of all visitors. Watch the right-side bins grow over time as a sign of repeat engagement.
  • Day-by-Day: Per-day rows (newest first) with the date, total answered, correct / wrong split, percent-correct bar, and the question text for that day. If a question was disabled or deleted after appearing on the daily slot, the row still shows its text and ID for context.
  • Refresh: Pulls the latest counts from the server without leaving the panel.
  • What's stored: One record per visitor per date, keyed by a random localStorage-generated visitor ID (no name, no email). A re-submission for the same date overwrites the prior record. Daily answers also bump the per-question correct/wrong tally on the Answer Stats panel (same as any other play).

Leaderboard

Lists every entry on the global Employee Records leaderboard. Each row shows the player's name, score, accuracy percentage, difficulty, category, and date. Hard and Medium are separate ladders that each hold up to 100 entries. Within a ladder, entries are ranked by score first, then full-bank runs above ones that excluded Behind the Scenes, then most recent (a newer run wins an otherwise-equal tie). Hard always ranks above Medium - the "All" view stacks Hard first, then Medium below; the difficulty tabs drill into either ladder. A small "Excluded Behind the Scenes" note appears under a player's name when they posted that score with the Behind the Scenes category turned off. This admin tab lists every saved score - up to 300 total. Anything past a difficulty's top 100 is held in reserve: saved but not shown on the public board, marked with an amber "In reserve - not shown" pill (the summary line up top counts how many are on the board vs. in reserve). Reserve scores aren't lost - they flow back onto the board if the per-difficulty display limit is ever raised. Use the Remove button to delete entries with inappropriate usernames or fraudulent scores. Removals are permanent.

Export

Download the full question bank as a CSV file. You pick which columns to include (question, answer, category, difficulty, tags, type, enabled status, community ratings, etc.). Handy for backups, sharing with collaborators, or importing into a spreadsheet for bulk review. Deleted questions are excluded; disabled questions appear with their status marked.

How Data Works

Player-submitted data (ratings, votes, disputes, feedback) is saved to the server automatically when a player submits it. It's also cached in each player's browser for faster loading. Answer Stats are different: they live only on the server (data/answer-stats.json) and are incremented one answer at a time as players play, so the counts are an authoritative global tally rather than anything stored per-browser. The server JSON files in the data/ folder are the source of truth.

Admin changes (question edits, disabling questions, resolving disputes) also save to the server immediately. You don't need to manually sync anything.
